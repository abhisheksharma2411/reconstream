# ReconStream v1.0.2

The revision the submitted preprint is built from. The Artifact Availability
section of the paper names this tag and the archival DOI, so this release is
what makes that sentence true.

Archived on Zenodo under concept DOI
[10.5281/zenodo.23272623](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23272623), which
resolves to the latest archived version. The paper cites the concept DOI, so it
keeps pointing at the newest release without an edit.

**Contract.** S1 (no premature break), S2 (no premature match), L1
(decisiveness) and M1 (stability), stated against a declared per-source
lateness model. The checker scores each verdict on two independent axes —
admissibility, computable with no ground truth, and outcome, which needs it.

**Findings.**
- An idleness timeout of 5 ticks puts 5.42% of obligations into contract
  violation (95% CI [3.94, 6.92]) on traffic where the same job without it is
  exactly sound; violations vanish at the bank's posting period, as predicted.
- Under lateness shift every reconciler tested raises spurious breaks on
  between 18.3% and 23.0% of the credits the bank did settle, while contract
  violations stay at or below 1.01%.
- One horizon per source halves the break-latency tail at equal soundness
  (p99 224 vs 446 ticks), and is *not* cheaper at every cost setting.
- The contract runs as SQL: 4,395/4,395 agreement with the reference checker.
- An Apache Flink interval join written the ordinary way violates the contract
  on 14.9–20.1% of obligations. The ordering across join windows reproduces;
  the individual per-configuration rates do not, and E10 measures why.

**Artifact.** `make reproduce` runs clean-room end to end. **29 tests**;
`scripts/verify_claims.py` checks all **136 numeric claims** in the paper
against the raw results; `scripts/check_provenance.py` requires every result
file to name one clean revision; `scripts/check_prose_numbers.py` requires
every numeric literal in the prose to be backed by a results file.

Consumes FinalityBench (arXiv:2609.04706, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22262591) as a
library.

## What changed since v1.0.1

v1.0.1 existed only to mint the DOI: Zenodo cannot assign one until a release
exists, so the DOI could not be in the paper that the first release contained.
This release closes that loop — the paper now cites the concept DOI, names
v1.0.2 as its tag, and the archive, the results and the manuscript are one
revision.

## What changed since v1.0.0

- **The two sibling citations were out of date.** FinalityBench was cited as a
  software artifact and CausalLoss-Fin as a "manuscript in preparation". Both
  are now posted and are cited as arXiv:2609.04706 and arXiv:2609.25960, each
  with its Zenodo DOI.
- **The artifact statement promised a DOI that did not exist.** "An archival
  DOI is forthcoming" is replaced by the repository URL and this tag. A DOI is
  not promised unless it is minted.
- **`paper/arxiv_metadata.txt` was hand-maintained and had drifted** — it
  claimed 8 pages for a 9-page paper and carried a literal
  `10.5281/zenodo.PLACEHOLDER` in the Comments field, which would have gone
  into the submission form. It is now generated by `make arxiv`: page count
  read off the verified PDF, figure/table/proposition counts from the source.
  It also records why arXiv's External DOI field stays blank — that field is
  for a DOI of *this paper* in a journal or proceedings, and a Zenodo software
  DOI is not that.
- **`make arxiv` did not exist** although `PUBLISH.md` told you to run it.
  Added, and wired into `reproduce` so a stale archive cannot survive a
  reproduction. `PUBLISH.md` now names the single file to upload,
  `paper/reconstream-arxiv.tar.gz`, rather than the staging directory.
- **These release notes were stale**: 27 tests, 70 claims, and a lateness-shift
  range of 11.8–14.8% that no longer matched the paper's 18.3–23.0%.

## Known limitations

Workloads are synthetic, generated from FinalityBench's simulator. Two stream
processors are modelled and one, Apache Flink, is run; Kafka Streams is not
evaluated directly. The simulated watermark arms agree with Flink on 77.0% and
78.8% of obligations, so the frontier timing results are properties of the
models and the paper says so. The idleness proposition is proved and modelled
but not confirmed on the engine, because a bounded file source never idles in
processing time.
